September 2026 · ~90 min · one continuous case, built by you
01 · The Context

Maison Lavernay Is Losing the Next Generation

Where You Start

You're in Strategy & New Business at Maison Lavernay, a 118 year old French jewellery house. Fine jewellery, leather goods, fragrance. No logos, no discounts, ever.

What's Happening

The customer who used to become a Lavernay client now arrives seven years later than she did a decade ago. Under 35s have gone from 21% of revenue to 12%.

What You Build Today

One new product or service that brings that customer back — researched, cased, and prototyped by you, end to end.

02 · What We're Doing

One story, three tools.

Chat → Cowork → Code — the same project carried through by CLAUDE.md and memory, so nothing gets re-explained at the handoff.

03 · The Output

Four deliverables, one consistent story.

Claude Project
CLAUDE.md, house identity, performance data and your locked concept, organized in one folder Claude reads automatically.
/project + CLAUDE.md
Business Case
Volume, price, cost, acquisition and payback — shaped to your offer, assumptions stated explicitly.
.xlsx
Pitch Deck
Evidence and pitch in one deck, built from a reusable house-standards skill.
.pptx + skill
Landing Page
A working one-page site for the product you invented this morning — built next, in Claude Code.
.html

Prerequisites

  • Claude desktop app installed and signed in with your workshop account (claude.com/download)
  • Cowork mode enabled in the app
  • Claude for Chrome extension installed
  • Claude for Excel & PowerPoint extension installed
  • The workshop folder downloaded, to use as your project workspace
Pro tip: Cowork will ask for permission before it reads or writes files outside the folder you point it to, before running shell commands, and before installing anything new. Approve each action, or tell it to do something different — you stay in control the whole time.

Core Steps

1
Set Up Your Workspace
What you'll learn: configuring Cowork, connecting your tools, and creating your first skill.
▾

Before touching the case, get Cowork ready:

  • Open Cowork's general settings — enable memory, and set tool access to "load tools when needed"
  • Connect the connectors you'll want later (Gmail at minimum)
  • Confirm the Claude for Chrome and Claude for Excel/PowerPoint extensions are installed
  • Create a write-like-me skill so anything Cowork drafts for you later sounds like you
Look at my last 10 to 20 sent emails and create a skill that captures my writing style. Focus on tone, sentence length, phrases I use and avoid. So future drafts sound like me.
2
Research the Shift (Claude Chat)
What you'll learn: opening a case in the browser and letting Claude read and cite live sources.
▾
Chat

This part happens in a regular claude.ai chat — no files, no desktop app yet.

# Role Act as a seasoned Marketing Professional for the Luxury Industry. I need you to help me plan the creation of a new product. # Instructions Use these two articles as reference: https://insights.ehl.edu/gen-z-luxury-consumption https://www.pandcglobal.com/research-insights/millennials-gen-z-are-transforming-luxury-retail/ Give me a market brief on what is actually happening to luxury demand under 35: the hard numbers, if the two sources disagree, and which claims I should not repeat to a committee without checking. Then follow their citations and pull two figures neither article states directly. # Output Produce me a concise markdown document as output

Download the markdown file. You'll bring this into Cowork in the next step — the concept itself gets decided there, once the house's own numbers are in the room.

3
Move from Chat to Cowork
What you'll learn: turning a research brief into a Cowork project, with CLAUDE.md doing the work Instructions usually would.
▾
Cowork starts here
  • Add the downloaded market brief to the workshop folder, inside /research
  • In the desktop app, create a project using Use local folder and choose the workshop folder
  • Do not add Instructions — you'll use the local CLAUDE.md file instead
Why this matters: without CLAUDE.md, every new session starts from zero and re-asks questions you've already answered.
4
Find Your Concept, Then Grill It
What you'll learn: shaping an idea against real company context, then pressure-testing it with the grill-me skill.
▾
Cowork

Everything from here runs on the folder. The house identity, the five year performance file, and your market brief are all in the room now.

Based on the market brief in the research folder: give me 3 different offers (new product line or a digital product / service) a 118 year old French jewellery house could launch to bring back the customer it is losing. Make them genuinely different from each other. For each, say in one line what it assumes about the customer that the others do not.

Pick one. Then install the grill-me skill from the folder and push on it:

I'm taking [the one you picked]. Grill me on it. What would have to be true, what am I assuming without evidence, and what is the version of this that a competitor could copy in six months?
Tip: if the grill-me session is deviating too much from what you want to do, just steer it. Ask Claude to stop once you feel you've reached an understanding.

When the idea holds:

Stop grilling me. Save the idea in the research folder, and update your project memory.
This is the whole context argument in one step. The same kind of question you could have asked in the browser becomes a sharper one here, because the house's brand rules and five years of its own numbers are sitting in the room with you.
5
See Who's Already Doing It
What you'll learn: using the Claude for Chrome extension to research and capture live references.
▾
Cowork
Use Chrome to look at two brands already doing something adjacent to my concept. Not competitors necessarily. I want to know how they explain it on their own site, how they price it, and what they got wrong. Screenshot and save into the project.
No Chrome extension? Ask Claude to use its built-in browser instead — it can navigate the pages and bring back what it finds.
6
Build the Business Case
What you'll learn: generating a spreadsheet with stated assumptions, then finishing it inside Excel.
▾
Cowork
Build the business case in Excel for my concept following /brand/business-case-standards.md. Keep it simple. There is no template, build the model the offer actually needs. Ground every assumption you can in the performance file or the research, and where you cannot, say so in the cell rather than making it look solid.

Open it in Excel and keep working there:

Add the sensitivity on the two inputs that move the answer most. Then tell me which single assumption the whole case rests on.
Important: the Claude extension in Excel and PowerPoint does not see your whole project — only what's inside the open workbook. Keep that in mind when asking it to reference other files.

Optional — leave something running:

Set up a weekly task that watches for anything that would change a number in my business case: category data, competitor launches in this space, resale platform news. Short. Only tell me when something actually moved.
7
Build the Pitch Deck + a Skill
What you'll learn: turning house standards into a reusable skill, then building the deck from it.
▾
Cowork
Turn /brand/deck-standards.md and the voice rules in /brand/house-identity.md into a reusable skill called lavernay-deck that anyone on the team can install. It should enforce the structure, the action-title rule, the sourcing rule and the banned words. It should assume nothing about my particular concept. Use /skill-creator to create it.

Once the skill exists, build the deck:

Using the lavernay-deck skill, build the committee deck for the idea on reserach folder . Evidence and pitch in one deck, not two. Pull every number from the model so the deck and the model cannot disagree. Slide 7 is the assumptions the recommendation depends on, stated so someone can argue with them.

Open it in PowerPoint to finish it:

Rewrite every slide title as a full sentence stating the finding. Then tell me which slide gets attacked first.
8
Hand Off to Claude Code: Build the Landing Page
What you'll learn: moving the same project into Claude Code and shipping a working prototype.
▾
Code
Build the landing page for my offer as a single HTML page, following /brand/landing-page-standards.md , but usign an interactivity similar to this website: https://www.igloo.inc/. House palette, no logo, one action — a way to register interest. Keep it simple, one page, no login, no backend. Create it under a folder named website.

This is where the Cowork track ends and the Claude Code track picks up — same project, same context, no re-explaining.

What "done" looks like
  • A Claude project with CLAUDE.md, house identity, performance data and your locked concept
  • A business case (.xlsx) with stated assumptions and a sensitivity table
  • A lavernay-deck skill anyone on your team could install tomorrow
  • A pitch deck (.pptx) whose numbers match the model
  • A working landing page for something that did not exist this morning

Troubleshooting

  • Something looks off — describe exactly what's wrong and what you expected instead; ask Cowork to redo just that piece, not the whole project.
  • Claude can't see what you're looking at — take a screenshot and paste it in (Cmd+V / Ctrl+V).
  • Excel/PowerPoint extension seems to be missing context — reminder: it only reads the open workbook, not your whole project folder.
  • Losing track across sessions — ask Cowork to summarize what it currently knows from CLAUDE.md and memory before continuing.
