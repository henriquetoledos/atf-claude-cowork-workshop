Maison Lavernay is losing the next generation.

September 2026 · about 2 hours · one offer, taken from idea to prototype

You have the research, the house's own numbers, and two hours. You decide what Lavernay should launch. By the end you will have a business case, a committee deck, and a working prototype of the thing you invented.

THE BRIEF

A 118 year old house whose customer now arrives twenty years late.

Where you start

You are in Strategy & New Business at Maison Lavernay. Paris, 1908. Fine jewellery, leather goods, fragrance. Family controlled, 190 boutiques, no logos, no discounts, ever.

What is happening

The entry tier has fallen from 34% of transactions to 19%. Average age at first purchase went from 36 to 41. Under 35s are now 12% of revenue, down from 21%. Revenue fell for the first time since 2009.

What you owe the committee

One new offer that brings that customer back. A product line, a service, a membership, a programme. Not a campaign for what you already sell.

Maison Lavernay is invented. The market research is real and published. The house, its numbers and its brand are ours, so you can argue about them freely.
HOW THE DAY RUNS

One question in the browser. Everything else in the folder.

Step 1 runs at claude.ai in a browser, with no files and no folder. It produces one document. From step 2 onward you work in Cowork on a local folder, because everything after the research needs the house's brand and financials in front of it.

Step 1claude.ai in a browser. Do not open the desktop app yet.
Steps 2 to 8Claude desktop app, Cowork, on the workshop folder.

What each step teaches

The ideas from the first session are not separate from the exercise. Each one has a moment below where you will see it happen.

IdeaWhere you meet it

Before you start

  • Claude desktop app installed and signed in with your workshop account (claude.com/download)
  • Cowork enabled in the app, memory on, tool access set to load tools when needed
  • Claude for Chrome extension installed
  • Claude for Excel and PowerPoint extensions installed
  • The workshop folder downloaded and unzipped somewhere you can find it
  • A browser tab open at claude.ai, signed in to the same account
If an extension will not install, raise your hand now rather than at step 3. Someone will come to you. Steps 3, 4 and 6 each use a different one.
On permissions: Cowork asks before it reads or writes outside the folder you pointed it at, before it runs anything in a shell, and before it installs. Approve, or redirect it. You stay in control throughout.

The steps

1
Research the shift
Browser only. One question, one document, then you are done with Chat.
▾
claude.ai in your browser

Do not open the desktop app for this step. Go to claude.ai in a browser tab.

# Role Act as a seasoned Marketing Professional for the Luxury Industry. I need you to help me plan the creation of a new product. # Instructions Use these two articles as reference: https://insights.ehl.edu/gen-z-luxury-consumption https://www.pandcglobal.com/research-insights/millennials-gen-z-are-transforming-luxury-retail/ Give me a market brief on what is actually happening to luxury demand under 35: the hard numbers, if the two sources disagree, and which claims I should not repeat to a committee without checking. Then follow their citations and pull two figures neither article states directly. # Output Produce me a concise markdown document as output

Then do these three things before moving on:

  • Download the markdown file
  • Put it in the workshop folder, inside /research
  • Open the desktop app, create a project with Use local folder, and choose the workshop folder
Why we stop here: Chat is for work you read once and carry away. The moment you want to keep building on something, you are in the wrong tool.
2
Find your opportunity
Now in Cowork, because the idea needs the brand and the numbers next to it.
▾
Claude desktop app · Cowork

Everything from here runs on the folder. The brand identity, the house's five year performance and your market brief are all in there now.

Based on the market brief in the folder: give me 3 different offers (new product line or a digital product / service) a 118 year old French jewellery house could launch to bring back the customer it is losing. Make them genuinely different from each other. For each, say in one line what it assumes about the customer that the others do not.

Pick one. Then push on it:

I'm taking [the one you picked]. Grill me on it. What would have to be true, what am I assuming without evidence, and what is the version of this that a competitor could copy in six months?

Go a few rounds. When the idea holds:

Stop grilling me. Save the idea in the /research folder, and update your project memory.
This is the difference between the two tools. The same question you could have asked in the browser becomes a better question here, because the house's brand rules and five years of its numbers are in the room with you.
3
See who is already doing it
Out to the live web, and back with evidence.
▾
Cowork Claude for Chrome
Use Chrome to look at two brands already doing something adjacent to my concept. Not competitors necessarily. I want to know how they explain it on their own site, how they price it, and what they got wrong. Screenshot and save into the project.
No Chrome extension? Ask Claude to use the built-in browser instead. It will navigate the pages and bring back what it finds.
4
Build the business case
No template. The model takes the shape your offer needs.
▾
Cowork Claude for Excel
Build the business case in Excel for my concept following /brand/business-case-standards.md. Keep it simple. There is no template, build the model the offer actually needs. Ground every assumption you can in the performance file or the research, and where you cannot, say so in the cell rather than making it look solid.

Open it in Excel and keep working there:

Add the sensitivity on the two inputs that move the answer most. Then tell me which single assumption the whole case rests on.
Note what just happened. Nobody gave you a spreadsheet template. A standards file described what a good business case contains, and the model was built to fit your offer rather than someone else's. That is the difference between a document a team has to remember and a standard that applies itself.
Worth knowing: the Excel and PowerPoint extensions only see the file you have open. They do not see the rest of your project folder. Ask them about the workbook in front of you, not about the deal.
5
Turn the house rules into a skill
The most reusable five minutes of the day.
▾
Cowork

Right now the deck standards are a document somebody has to remember to read. Make them something that applies itself.

Turn /brand/deck-standards.md and the voice rules in /brand/house-identity.md into a reusable skill called lavernay-deck that anyone on the team can install. It should enforce the structure, the action-title rule, the sourcing rule and the banned words. It should assume nothing about my particular concept. Use /skill-creator to create it.
This is the answer to "how do we scale this". Not a longer prompt. A standard that installs itself, once, for everybody.
6
Build the committee deck
Evidence and pitch in one deck, every number traceable.
▾
Cowork Claude for PowerPoint
Using the lavernay-deck skill, build the committee deck. Evidence and pitch in one deck, not two. Pull every number from the model so the deck and the model cannot disagree. Slide 7 is the assumptions the recommendation depends on, stated so someone can argue with them.

Then open it in PowerPoint:

Rewrite every slide title as a full sentence stating the finding. Then tell me which slide gets attacked first.
7
Build the prototype
The thing you actually walk out with.
▾
Cowork
Build the landing page for my offer as a single self-contained HTML file, following /brand/landing-page-standards.md exactly. House palette, no logo, one action. Open it so I can see it.
An hour ago this was a paragraph you wrote in a browser. Nobody wrote a line of code.
8
Leave something running
The work does not stop when you close the laptop.
▾
Cowork
Set up a weekly task that watches for anything that would change a number in my business case: category data, competitor launches in this space, resale platform news. Short. Only tell me when something actually moved.
Everything before this step was you working faster. This step is work happening without you.
What you should have by now
  • A market brief you pulled in the browser and carried into the folder
  • Your offer, saved and in the project's memory
  • Benchmark screenshots from two brands doing something adjacent
  • A business case (.xlsx) shaped to your offer, with a sensitivity
  • A lavernay-deck skill anyone on your team could install tomorrow
  • A committee deck (.pptx) whose numbers match the model
  • A working landing page for something that did not exist this morning
  • A weekly task that keeps watching

If something breaks

  • Cowork cannot see your files. Check which folder the project points at. It only reads inside that folder.
  • The Excel or PowerPoint extension seems lost. It only sees the open file, never the project. Give it what it needs in the prompt.
  • The Chrome extension will not install. Ask Claude to use the built-in browser instead.
  • Claude cannot see what you are looking at. Screenshot it and paste it in.
  • The output is generic. That is almost always a context problem, not a prompt problem. Ask it which files it actually read.
  • You lost the thread between sessions. Ask it to summarise what it currently knows from CLAUDE.md and memory before you continue.
  • It made a number up. Ask for the source file and row. Then tell it to flag the gap instead of filling it.
